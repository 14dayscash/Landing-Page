## What It Actually Takes to List a House on the Market

Buyers using financing need the house to pass inspection and appraisal. That means a real list of repairs most sellers do not budget for until they are already three showings in and getting feedback.

- **Roof.** If it is near the end of its life or damaged, expect $5,000 to $13,000 for a replacement, averaging around $9,500 depending on size and material.
- **HVAC.** A full system replacement runs $6,000 to $15,500 in most cases, and can climb toward $28,000 for larger homes or high-efficiency systems. FHA and VA loans specifically require working heat and air, or the inspector will flag.
- **Electrical and plumbing.** Older panels, knob-and-tube wiring, or failing galvanized pipe are common inspection flags in older homes, and buyers' lenders will not close until they are addressed.
- **Foundation and structural issues.** Even minor settling cracks can trigger a request for a structural engineer's report, which can stall a sale for weeks before repairs could even be started.
- **Paint, flooring, and cosmetic work.** Not required by any lender, but competing against move-in-ready listings usually means doing it anyway.
- **Pest and termite clearance.** VA and many FHA loans require a clear termite report before closing. If there is active damage to the home, that repair happens before the buyer's loan can fund.
- **Landscaping and curb appeal.** The first thing every buyer and every appraiser sees before they walk in the door.

None of that includes the repairs that show up after the buyer's own inspector walks the house, which is its own negotiation most sellers are not expecting either.

## The Other Costs Nobody Mentions Upfront

Repairs are only part of it.

- **Agent commissions.** The 2026 average total commission is around 5.5%. On a house priced near Visalia's current median of roughly $400,000, that is $22,000 coming out of your proceeds at closing, not before repairs, after them.
- **A more complicated commission conversation than it used to be.** Since the NAR settlement changes, buyer-agent compensation is now negotiated separately rather than automatically bundled into the listing, which means sellers are having a new conversation about who pays which commission.
- **Closing costs.** Title, escrow, and transfer taxes typically run another 1% to 2% of the sale price on top of commission.
- **Holding costs while it sits.** Every month on the market is another mortgage payment, insurance premium, property tax installment, and utility bill, on top of upkeep for a vacant or occupied-but-stressed house that needs to sell.
- **The risk that it falls through anyway.** Financed buyers can lose their loan approval, low appraisals can reopen the price, and a bad inspection can send you back to repair negotiations you thought were behind you. Be cautious!

Add up repairs, commission, closing costs, and a few months of holding costs, and a house that nets $370,000 on paper can land tens of thousands of dollars lower by the time everything is actually paid.
