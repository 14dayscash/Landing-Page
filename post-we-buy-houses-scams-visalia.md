House Junkies gets compared to every other "we buy houses" text, postcard, and bandit sign in Visalia constantly, so let's talk about why that comparison usually falls apart the moment someone asks a few basic questions. Most of what is flooding your mailbox right now is not a buyer at all. It is someone hoping to lock up your house long enough to sell the contract to someone else.

## The Tell Most Sellers Miss: No Contract Ready

Here is the simplest test there is. Ask whoever is sitting across from you to show you a purchase agreement, right now, with real terms on it. A real buyer has one ready, because writing offers is the entire job. Most wholesalers do not. They are still trying to figure out if they can find someone else to buy the contract before they commit to anything in writing. If the person in your living room cannot produce an actual contract on the spot, that alone tells you most of what you need to know.

## Other Red Flags Worth Watching For

- **No proof of funds.** A real cash buyer can show you bank statements or a proof of funds letter without hesitation. A wholesaler usually cannot, because there is no cash, there is a plan to find someone else's cash.
- **They will not answer a direct question about closing.** Ask plainly: "Are you the one actually closing on this house, or are you planning to sell this contract to someone else?" A legitimate buyer answers that in one sentence. A wholesaler gets vague, changes the subject, or gives you an answer that does not actually answer the question.
- **No physical office you can verify.** Look up the address. Is it a real office, or a UPS Store box, or nothing at all?
- **High pressure, today-only urgency.** Real offers do not expire in six hours. That pressure exists to stop you from checking anything.
- **The price drops after "inspection."** A lot of these outfits never intended to pay the number they opened with. The plan was always to get you comfortable, then come back lower once you have mentally moved on.
- **Nothing to verify.** No completed deals you can look up, no reviews, no social presence beyond a single landing page and a phone number.

## How Assignment Clauses Actually Work, and How They Get Misused

Almost every purchase contract, ours included, has a clause that allows the named buyer to assign their rights under that contract to another party before closing. On its own, that clause is completely normal and completely legal. Assigning a contract to an affiliated entity, a JV partner, or a different piece of your own company for financing or structuring reasons is standard practice, and a legitimate operation still fully intends to close no matter which entity ends up on the deed.

Here is where it goes wrong. A lot of wholesalers use that same clause with no capital and no real intention of closing themselves. They get a property under contract, then spend the next two or three weeks trying to find some other investor willing to pay them a fee to take that contract off their hands. If they find someone, the deal moves forward, usually with you never having met or vetted whoever the actual end buyer turns out to be. If they do not find someone, the deal collapses, sometimes deep into escrow, and you are back where you started, except weeks behind on whatever timeline you were working with.

Under current California law, wholesaling itself is legal. A person can market their contractual interest in a property without holding a real estate license, as long as they are not acting as an unlicensed broker. That said, the state legislature has been actively looking at this. AB 1850, introduced in early 2026 with backing from the California Association of Realtors, would require wholesalers to hold a real estate license and disclose in writing, upfront, that they do not intend to take title to the property. As of this writing that bill has not passed, but it tells you the state has noticed the same pattern sellers keep running into.

The honest version of this business discloses upfront who is actually buying and closing. The version that scams people hides that question for as long as possible.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is wholesaling real estate illegal in California?**
No. Wholesaling itself is legal under current California law, as long as the wholesaler is marketing their contractual interest and not acting as an unlicensed real estate broker. The concern for sellers is not that assignment happens, it is when it happens without disclosure.

**What is an assignment of contract in real estate?**
It is a clause that lets the buyer named in a purchase agreement transfer their rights under that contract to another party before closing. Legitimate companies use it for financing and entity structuring. Wholesalers sometimes use it to shop a contract to strangers with no capital of their own behind it.

**How can I tell if someone is a real buyer or a wholesaler?**
Ask directly whether they are the one closing, ask for proof of funds, and ask to see a real purchase contract on the spot. A real buyer answers all three without hesitation.

**Does House Junkies ever assign contracts?**
We do not shop your contract to unknown third parties looking for someone to buy it from us. We are the buyer, we close with our own capital, and our construction and brokerage arms are part of the same company, not separate parties we are hoping to bring in later.

**What happens if a wholesaler cannot find a buyer for my contract?**
The deal typically falls through, sometimes weeks into what you thought was an active escrow. You are left having lost time you cannot get back, occasionally on a deadline that mattered.
